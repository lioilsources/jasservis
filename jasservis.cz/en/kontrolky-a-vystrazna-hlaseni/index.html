VINIdentifying my vehicle – VIN
VIN

What a VIN is

A VIN is a unique vehicle identifier made up of 17 VIN characters. Characters 1–3 form the World Manufacturer Identifier (WMI), characters 4–9 the Vehicle Descriptor Section (VDS), and characters 10–17 the Vehicle Identifier Section (VIS). The exact meaning of individual positions can vary by manufacturer and market, so a VIN alone does not establish the complete equipment or service specification without the matching records.

Vehicle documents

In a registration certificate, look for field E or wording that identifies the vehicle identification number (VIN). Older and newer certificates, and documents issued in different countries, use different layouts, so one fixed position cannot be stated for every document.

Where the VIN may be found on the vehicle

Some versions display the VIN in a field visible from outside through the windscreen. The VIN is also marked directly on the body; its exact location depends on the model, generation and body version, not only the brand. Follow the manual for the exact vehicle and compare the number with its documents.

Labels and service documents

Where fitted, a vehicle data sticker may be located in the luggage compartment or service documents. A vehicle data sticker containing vehicle codes is different from the manufacturer’s plate carrying approval and production data and from the VIN marked directly on the body.

Examples of initial identifiers

Documented examples of WMI identifiers for the selected brand include: WVW, WVG. These are examples, not a complete list. The identifier alone does not establish the exact engine, equipment or complete vehicle version; the full VIN and matching documentation are required.

Data stored in the key

In some vehicles, identification data and selected service information are also stored in the key. A workshop with suitable equipment can read them with a specialised reader. The availability and scope of the data depend on the vehicle model and key version.

Engine oil pressure

Red · Warning · As described in the source

Meaning

The warning indicates insufficient engine oil pressure. A correct oil level alone does not confirm adequate lubrication pressure.

Recommended action

Stop safely and switch off the engine. Do not leave it idling. Further steps, including any restart, must follow the specific vehicle manual; if unsure, obtain professional assistance.

Key indication

Do not confuse this with a yellow oil level warning. The source describes a conditional procedure after topping up; this is not general permission to restart.

Combustion engine cooling system

Red · Warning · As described in the source

Meaning

May indicate overheating, an incorrect coolant level or a cooling system fault.

Recommended action

Stop safely, switch off the engine and follow the vehicle message. Check coolant as instructed only after safe cooling. Do not open a hot expansion tank.

Key indication

Visible coolant in the reservoir does not by itself resolve the warning. This entry does not cover electric-drive cooling.

Engine management and emissions – MIL

Yellow / amber · Warning · As described in the source

Meaning

The control system detected an engine management or emissions fault. The lamp alone does not identify a failed component.

Recommended action

Distinguish steady illumination from flashing. On relevant versions, flashing can indicate misfiring. If the engine shakes, vibrates or loses power markedly, stop safely and contact professional assistance. Otherwise follow the message and vehicle manual.

Key indication

Clearing a fault code does not remove the cause. This entry does not give universal permission to continue driving.

Diesel particulate filter – DPF

Yellow / amber · Warning · As described in the source

Meaning

On relevant diesel versions it indicates accumulated soot and a need for filter regeneration.

Recommended action

Check regeneration driving conditions in your specific manual. If other warnings appear or engine operation is abnormal, do not follow a universal internet regeneration procedure.

Key indication

No single speed, duration or engine speed applies to all engines. Petrol GPF/OPF requires separate verification.

Fuses – colours and ratings

Has the fuse blown again? Do not try another replacement.

If a new fuse of the specified type and current rating blows again, do not insert another one. Have the cause of the fault identified by a qualified workshop. Possible causes include a short circuit, an overloaded electrical circuit or a fault in a connected device.

Never use a “stronger” fuse with a higher ampere rating. Do not repair a fuse or bridge it with wire or any other object.

Doing so defeats the protection of the electrical circuit and can cause overheating and damage to wiring, connectors or electronic components. In an extreme case, the vehicle could catch fire.

A replacement fuse must always match the type, dimensions and current rating specified by the vehicle manufacturer.

Automotive fuse types

Identify the fuse construction before selecting a replacement. A matching colour in a different fuse family does not establish the same design.

Selecting the correct fuse

Switch off the relevant consumer and ignition as instructed. Use only the same type and rating; repeated failure requires diagnosis.

Where to find the tyre-pressure placard

The exact location varies by model, generation, body version and market. Look for the placard on the vehicle, not on the tyre. Common documented locations include:

  1. Fuel filler flapOn the inside of the fuel filler flap where the particular version uses this location.
  2. B-pillar or door frameIn the door aperture, visible when a front door is open. The side of the vehicle can vary.
  3. Front doorOn the edge or inner face of a front door on some versions.
  4. Vehicle manualIf the placard cannot be found, verify its exact location in the manual for the specific model. Do not assume an electric vehicle has a fuel-filler location.

On the placard, match the tyre size, front and rear axle values, and load condition. Values may be stated in bar, kPa or psi and may also include the spare wheel.

Why the pressure must match the vehicle

Use the pressure specified by the vehicle manufacturer for the exact tyre size, axle and load. “MAX PRESS” on the sidewall is a tyre limit, not the vehicle’s normal recommended pressure.

  • Check pressure when the tyres are cold and follow the vehicle placard or manual. Front and rear axles, and normal and full loads, may require different values.
  • Underinflation increases flexing and heat, can impair steering and braking, accelerate shoulder wear and increase fuel or energy consumption.
  • Overinflation can reduce the effective contact area, impair comfort and grip on uneven surfaces, and increase susceptibility to damage or uneven wear.
  • Ambient temperature changes pressure. A tyre-pressure monitoring system helps detect a deviation but does not replace regular measurement and visual inspection.

Fuels

Choose fuel according to the requirements of the exact engine. The prescribed standard, octane rating and approved fuel composition govern.

Which fuel belongs in my vehicle?

The information is usually inside the fuel-filler flap and in the owner’s manual. A model name or the designation TSI, TFSI or TDI alone is not enough for an exact choice.

For older vehicles, verify E10 compatibility. Higher-octane petrol is not automatically E5; always check the pump label.

Petrol – what does the octane rating mean?

The octane rating describes petrol’s resistance to combustion knock. Europe uses the RON designation, for example 95, 98 or 100.

Examples of vehicle label information

A recommended value and a permitted minimum are not the same. Emergency use of lower-octane fuel is governed solely by the exact vehicle manual.

98 or 100 petrol can benefit engines able to use its properties. A higher octane rating alone does not guarantee more power or lower consumption in every vehicle.

Diesel – what does the cetane number mean?

The cetane number describes diesel fuel’s ability to ignite after injection into hot compressed air. A higher value generally means a shorter ignition delay and can benefit cold starting and combustion noise.

Diesel meeting EN 590 has a minimum cetane number of 51. It is not the only quality measure; cleanliness, lubricity and low-temperature properties also matter.

Why avoid regularly driving into the reserve?

We recommend refuelling in good time, ideally before the reserve warning comes on.

DPF regeneration
Depending on the vehicle, a low fuel level can restrict or interrupt regeneration. If both the DPF and reserve warnings are on, refuel before attempting the prescribed regeneration drive.
Fuel-operated auxiliary heater
At a low fuel level it may not start or may switch off early. This need not indicate a heater fault. The vehicle manual defines the exact conditions.

Do not run the tank completely dry. Fuel exhaustion can cause irregular running or stop the engine.

Do fuel additives make sense?

Suitable additive treatment can have benefits. Fuel treated by its producer and an additive poured into the tank later are not automatically interchangeable.

Possible benefits of suitable treatmentRisks of unsuitable use
Keeping the fuel system clean and limiting deposits.An unapproved product can damage the engine or emissions system.
Protection against corrosion.Metallic octane additives can damage the engine and catalytic converter.

Effects depend on the fuel composition and exact additive and cannot be promised for every product.

Use aftermarket additives only where the manufacturer permits them for the exact vehicle. Some manuals expressly prohibit adding them to diesel. Follow the dose for an approved product and do not combine additives without verifying compatibility.

For winter operation, use diesel suitable for the expected temperatures. Never dilute diesel with petrol.

J&S SERVIS recommendation: Start with fuel that meets the vehicle specification, refuel in good time and maintain the vehicle regularly. Assess an aftermarket additive for the exact engine and intended purpose.
Have you filled with the wrong fuel?
Do not start the engine; contact a workshop or roadside assistance. If the engine is already running, stop safely and switch it off. Further driving can greatly increase the damage.
Oils – viscosity and specifications
How to select the correct engine oil

Engine oils for Volkswagen, Audi, Škoda, SEAT and CUPRA

The correct engine oil must match the exact engine and manufacturer requirements. “5W-30” or “0W-20” alone is insufficient: the prescribed standard and oil approval matter as well as viscosity.

Requirements for one model can vary by engine, production period and service regime. Select oil using the VIN, exact engine and service documentation.

What does SAE viscosity mean?

Viscosity describes resistance to flow. The SAE designation describes behaviour at low and high temperatures:

  • The number before W, such as 0W or 5W, is the low-temperature grade relevant to cold starting and pumpability.
  • W comes from the English word Winter.
  • The number after the hyphen, such as 20, 30 or 40, is the high-temperature viscosity grade.

These numbers are neither ambient-temperature values nor a quality scale. A 5W-40 oil is not automatically more suitable than 0W-20. Engine design and the manufacturer requirement govern.

VW standards: an indicative overview

Standards such as VW 504 00, VW 507 00 and VW 508 00 define oil-performance and application requirements.

Two oils with equal viscosity can have different approvals and need not be interchangeable. General ACEA or API designations do not replace the required VW standard unless the manufacturer expressly permits an alternative for the situation.

Check the exact approval of the product. Vehicle-manufacturer approval differs from an oil producer’s recommendation.

Low level, checking and topping up

Check the level by the procedure specified for the exact vehicle. Oil temperature, vehicle position, time since engine shutdown and other conditions can affect electronic measurement.

Add oil of the prescribed specification gradually and as directed by the manual or vehicle. Do not exceed the permitted level.

Some manuals permit a limited emergency top-up with an alternative when the prescribed oil is unavailable. The quantity and specification apply only to that vehicle and situation, not universally across Group vehicles.

Audi: updating the oil level after topping up

On some Audi models the engine-oil level in the MMI may update only after the bonnet has been closed and the ignition switched on again. Also meet the measurement conditions in the exact vehicle manual.

If the value does not change immediately, do not automatically add more oil. First verify the measurement conditions.

Rising oil level

Is the engine-oil level rising during use? Simply extracting the excess may not be enough.

If the oil level was correct after a change or top-up and then rises during subsequent use, the cause must be identified. Particular attention is required if the vehicle asks for the level to be reduced even though no oil has been added in the meantime. Fuel entering the engine oil is one possible cause.

How this may relate to the diesel particulate filter (DPF)

During active DPF regeneration, some diesel engines use additional fuel injection to raise exhaust-gas temperature. Some of this fuel can enter the oil fill.

Frequent short trips, insufficient engine warm-up and repeatedly interrupted regenerations can contribute to fuel accumulating in the oil. The connection should also be investigated if regenerations occur unusually often.

A high-level message alone does not confirm a DPF fault. Fuel dilution of the oil can also occur in petrol engines, for example after repeated short trips.

Why merely reducing the level is not enough

Excessive fuel dilution changes the oil’s properties and can reduce protection for lubricated engine components.

Extracting only the excess corrects the level, but the remaining oil stays diluted. This does not restore its original lubricating properties.

If excessive dilution or other oil degradation is confirmed, arrange an oil change without delay.

We recommend replacing both the engine oil and oil filter in accordance with the manufacturer procedure while also investigating the cause. Do not wait for the normal oil-change interval in this situation.

Changing the oil alone does not rectify an underlying fault. The problem can recur unless its cause is identified.

What the driver should do

  • Do not add any more oil.
  • Contact a workshop without unnecessary delay. State the exact message, when the oil was last changed or topped up, and whether the level rose gradually.
  • Base any further driving on the exact vehicle message and its manual; agree the appropriate course of action with the workshop.
  • Do not try to address a high level and suspected oil dilution by taking a long drive to “burn off” the DPF or by initiating a forced regeneration yourself without inspection.
  • If STOP is displayed, driving is prohibited, or low oil pressure is reported, stop safely, switch off the engine and obtain qualified assistance.

Overfilling and a rising level are different situations

If too much of the correct oil was added during a change or top-up and the fill has not degraded, a workshop may adjust its quantity.

If the level rises during use without further oil being added, the condition cannot automatically be treated as simple overfilling. A workshop must verify the true level, the measurement method and the condition of the oil fill.

Low oil pressure

For a red low-oil-pressure warning, stop safely and switch off the engine. Do not confuse it with a low-level warning. Low pressure can occur even when enough oil is present. Follow the exact vehicle instruction and obtain qualified assistance.

Transmission and other oils

Assess manual transmissions, DSG, other automatics, transfer cases, differentials and all-wheel-drive clutches separately for their exact construction. Engine-oil standards or similar viscosity do not establish suitability.

To identify the correct oil we need the complete VIN and, where necessary, engine and service-regime details. We verify the prescribed standard, suitable viscosity and fill for the exact vehicle version.

Mixing and frost protection

A product marked “ready mix” is prepared for use and must not be diluted further with water.

Concentrate must be mixed with the specified water. For this guidance use distilled water; use other water only where the manufacturer expressly permits it.

The ratio depends on the exact product, vehicle and lowest expected operating temperature. Neat concentrate does not automatically provide the best protection; exceeding the specified proportion can impair cooling and frost protection.

A 1:1 ratio does not universally mean −35 °C or −40 °C. Conversion from ratio to temperature must be documented for the exact concentrate; a G12++/G13 table is not a universal G12evo instruction.

Actual frost protection depends on the original fill and previous top-ups. If uncertain, have the coolant checked with suitable measuring equipment by a workshop.

Hybrids and electric vehicles – sealed reservoirs

Some hybrid and electric vehicles use several separate cooling circuits. Some versions have two expansion reservoirs, one of which is sealed. Their number and purpose depend on the exact vehicle.

Do not open a sealed reservoir, break its seal, or add coolant or water. Its level may only be checked visually as stated by the vehicle manual. Customer top-up is permitted only for a circuit that the manufacturer allows for the exact vehicle.

If the level falls in a sealed circuit, a workshop must find the cause, check for leaks, and determine repair and filling under the manufacturer procedure.

Checking the level and topping up

This procedure applies only to a reservoir that the manufacturer permits the customer to top up on the exact vehicle. It does not apply to a sealed or workshop-only circuit.

  1. Park on firm level ground, switch off the drive and ignition, and allow the system to cool completely. Do not open a hot or pressurised reservoir.
  2. Use the manual to identify the correct reservoir and specified coolant safely.
  3. Check the cold level against the reservoir marks. If it is between MIN and MAX, topping up is normally unnecessary.
  4. Top up only if coolant remains in the reservoir and the manual permits the procedure.
  5. Open the cap carefully. Add only new specified coolant and do not exceed the upper mark.
  6. Close the cap securely. Check the cold level again, for example on the following day.
Coolant loss and workshop assistance

A substantial or repeated coolant loss can indicate a leak or another fault. The cause and system tightness require qualified inspection; repeated topping up can conceal the fault.

If the reservoir is completely empty, do not continue driving or treat the situation as a simple top-up. With a red warning or STOP instruction, stop safely, follow the vehicle message and seek qualified assistance.

Do not add sealing products or unknown fluids. Do not judge compatibility of different specifications by colour or a general trade description.

Brake fluid

Brake fluid transmits hydraulic pressure to the brakes. The correct specification, level and condition are essential for safe braking. During routine service, J&S SERVIS checks the level and measures the brake fluid boiling point.

Which fluid belongs in Volkswagen Group vehicles?

Many Volkswagen, Audi, Škoda, SEAT and CUPRA vehicles specify low-viscosity brake fluid meeting VW 501 14.

A “DOT 4” label alone does not confirm suitability for every vehicle. The manufacturer’s exact specification governs. Low viscosity also supports rapid ABS and ESC operation at low temperatures.

Why do we measure the boiling point?

Brake fluid gradually absorbs moisture, which lowers its boiling point. It can then boil sooner during intensive braking.

The resulting compressible vapour bubbles can cause a soft pedal, increased pedal travel and a major reduction or loss of braking effect.

A measured boiling point around 180 °C or lower means reduced thermal reserve. We recommend preventive fluid replacement. The risk is most relevant under high heat load, such as prolonged braking on a descent. This is not a temperature at which the brakes automatically stop working.

We assess the result against the specified fluid. Even a good result does not replace a change due under the manufacturer’s schedule.

Why does the level gradually fall?

A slight fall can be associated with brake-pad wear. The pistons extend progressively, leaving more fluid in the calipers, so the reservoir level can fall without a leak.

A single need to top up does not by itself prove worn pads. Pad condition and system tightness must be checked. We do not automatically fill to MAX without finding the cause of the lower level.

Repeated topping up requires inspection

Brake fluid is not consumed in normal operation. A repeated or rapid fall can indicate a leak or another fault. Topping up alone does not solve the problem.

J&S SERVIS checks the cause of the loss, brake condition and tightness of the relevant hydraulic system.

Safety warning

With a red low brake-fluid warning or a sudden deterioration in braking effect, do not continue driving; arrange qualified assistance.

Washer fluids

The correct washer fluid maintains a clear view and protects the washer system. Select it for the season, expected temperatures and vehicle requirements.

In freezing conditions, warm the windscreen with the heater, ventilation or windscreen heating, depending on vehicle equipment, before using the washers. Fluid can freeze on cold glass and suddenly restrict visibility. Before operating the wipers, check that they are not frozen to the glass. This instruction is also stated in the manufacturer manual.

Preparing for winter: topping up the reservoir is not enough

Prepare the winter fill before the first frost:

  • Use up or have the remaining summer fluid extracted; do not run the pump dry.
  • Add winter fluid with suitable frost protection.
  • Operate the washers briefly to move the winter fluid into the lines and nozzles.
  • Remember the rear washer and any headlamp washers.

Remaining water or summer fluid reduces the resulting frost protection, so the value on the added container may not match the actual mixture in the vehicle. The old fill can also remain in the long line to the rear window.

What can freezing damage?

Frozen fluid can damage the reservoir, pump, hoses or their joints and cause a subsequent leak. Expanding ice can also rupture a line.

Repeatedly operating a blocked pump can damage it. On some vehicles the control unit can record a fault and restrict further pump operation.

What should be used for dilution and why follow the ratio?

Dilute concentrate exactly to the table on its container. A 1:1 ratio does not necessarily provide the same frost protection for different products.

Use ready-mixed fluid without further dilution unless the manufacturer expressly permits otherwise. Adding water changes cleaning performance and frost protection.

Select the water type under the instructions of the vehicle and fluid manufacturer. Clean water limits contamination of the system; suitability of hard water also depends on the product formulation.

Distilled or demineralised water is not a universal recommendation for every vehicle. Some Volkswagen manuals expressly do not permit distilled water.

Do not add engine coolant, washing-up liquid or other unspecified products to the washer system. An unsuitable combination can impair visibility or block the nozzles.
Why prefer summer fluid in summer?

Summer products principally target insects and other summer dirt. Winter fluids have a different formulation, and their antifreeze protection is not needed in warm weather.

A winter fluid suitable for the vehicle is not automatically harmful or prohibited in summer. Some concentrates also have a manufacturer-specified summer dilution. The exact product and its instructions govern.

Fluid rated to −20 °C at an outside temperature of −16 °C

Higher speed alone does not mean such a mixture must freeze. Moving air accelerates cooling but does not by itself cool the fluid below the actual ambient temperature. A small hose diameter does not change the mixture’s freezing point. Fluid in the reservoir behaves differently from a thin film on the glass: evaporation cools the thin film and also changes its composition.

Washer operation can still fail in frost. The actual dilution, residual water and suitability for the exact nozzles matter. Fill frost protection need not equal the temperature limit for correct atomisation by fan nozzles; a manufacturer may state both values separately.

What should you do if the washers freeze?
  • Do not operate the washers repeatedly.
  • Allow the whole system to thaw gradually in a space above freezing.
  • Do not use a heat gun or another intense heat source.
  • After thawing, adjust or replace the fill and verify operation and system tightness.
J&S SERVIS recommendation: Before winter, prepare fluid with an adequate temperature margin and verify suitability for the fitted nozzles. If the washers do not work and a safe view cannot be maintained, do not continue driving.

Emissions and maintenance

Exhaust-gas treatment – DPF/GPF, AdBlue

DPF and GPF/OPF filters trap particulate matter. AdBlue helps the SCR system reduce nitrogen-oxide emissions. Each system has a different function and requires a different response when a warning light comes on.

DPF and GPF/OPF – what does the warning light mean?

DPF is the particulate filter for diesel engines. GPF/OPF denotes the particulate filter for petrol engines.

In normal operation, trapped soot is progressively burned off during regeneration. Frequent short journeys can prevent completion. The filter warning light can therefore request a regeneration drive.

How can regeneration be supported while driving?

The engine must be warm. Always verify the exact procedure in your vehicle manual; speed, engine speed and driving time vary by engine.

Examples from manuals for selected Audi vehicles

FilterRecommended procedure
Diesel – DPFDrive at least 60 km/h at no less than 2,000 rpm. Regeneration can take approximately 20–40 minutes. The warning light going out indicates successful completion.
Petrol – GPF/OPFAccelerate to at least 80 km/h, then fully release the accelerator for several seconds and allow the vehicle to slow in gear. Repeat as directed by the manual until the light goes out.

Observe speed limits and adapt to traffic and weather. These examples are not universal instructions for every Volkswagen Group vehicle.

During regeneration, idle speed may rise, an odour may occur, or the fan may continue after the engine is switched off. Because of the high exhaust temperature, do not park over dry grass or other combustible material.
The light stayed on or fault symptoms appeared
  • If the light does not go out after driving as instructed, or repeatedly returns, book the vehicle for diagnosis.
  • If the engine fault or EPC warning is on, the glow-plug light flashes, the engine runs unevenly, or the vehicle has a substantial loss of power, do not attempt another regeneration drive. Contact a workshop or roadside assistance.
  • For a red warning, follow the vehicle instructions and stop safely.
  • If the fuel-reserve warning is also on, refuel before any regeneration drive and then follow the vehicle manual.
DPF service life – J&S SERVIS experience

In our workshop experience, the DPF in some diesel vehicles is near the end of its service life at around 250,000 km. This is not a fixed replacement interval.

Soot burns during regeneration, but non-combustible ash remains in the filter. Actual filter condition, diagnosis and the manufacturer procedure determine whether professional cleaning or replacement is appropriate. This mileage must not automatically be applied to petrol GPF/OPF filters.

How to top up AdBlue correctly
  1. Park on level ground and switch off the engine and ignition.
  2. Use ready-to-use AdBlue meeting ISO 22241-1.
  3. Observe the minimum and maximum refill quantity shown on the display or in the manual.
  4. When using a suitable filling nozzle, stop after its first automatic shut-off.
  5. After filling, follow the recognition procedure in the manual. On some vehicles, first switch on the ignition only and wait approximately 30 seconds before starting the engine.
Do not dilute AdBlue with water, add additives, or ever pour it into the fuel tank. If fluids are mixed up, do not start the engine; contact a workshop.
What happens if AdBlue is not topped up?

The vehicle warns of a low supply and displays the remaining range.

A running engine does not automatically switch off solely because AdBlue is exhausted. Once the displayed range reaches zero, however, the engine cannot be restarted after it is switched off. Do not postpone topping up until the final kilometres.

Topped up AdBlue but the message remains?

Recognition can take some time. On some vehicles, first switch on the ignition only and wait approximately 30 seconds before starting the engine. The procedure in the exact vehicle manual governs.

AdBlue depletion alone does not automatically mean that diagnostic start enabling is required. Add the prescribed quantity and follow the refill-recognition procedure in the manual. If the no-start restriction or warning remains after correct filling, contact a workshop. Depending on the version, diagnosis and the applicable adaptation under the manufacturer procedure may be required. An SCR/AdBlue system fault message must be distinguished from a low fluid level alone.
