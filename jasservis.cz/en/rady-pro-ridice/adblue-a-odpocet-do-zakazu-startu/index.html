What the message means and why topping up is sometimes not enough

Which vehicles this advice applies to

Volkswagen Group diesel vehicles with an SCR system. Warning messages, system design and repair options depend on the particular vehicle.

What to do now

First distinguish what the message means

What the vehicle reports How to deal with the situation
Low AdBlue supply Add the correct fluid in the quantity and using the procedure shown on the display and in your vehicle's handbook. Do not wait for the countdown to run out.
AdBlue or SCR fault Arrange a professional check. A fault message may remain even when there is enough fluid.
Another engine start is not possible Discuss the next steps with the workshop or roadside assistance according to the cause of the warning. Do not assume that repeated attempts to start will resolve the situation.

The countdown concerns the next engine start

Running out of AdBlue alone does not mean that the system automatically switches off a running engine when the countdown reaches zero. After the engine is switched off, however, it may prevent it from being started again. This information is not a recommendation to leave the engine running or wait until the final kilometres to deal with the problem.

If little distance remains in the countdown, agree on the next steps before another journey. Take account of other warnings and the vehicle's actual behaviour as well. Photograph the exact message only after stopping safely.

Use ready-to-use AdBlue meeting ISO 22241-1. Do not dilute it with water or add other additives. If AdBlue and fuel have been mixed up, do not start the engine and contact professional assistance.

What the symptom may mean

A message appears stating that another engine start will not be possible after the displayed distance. The cause may be a low AdBlue supply, but it may also be a system fault. The countdown alone therefore does not mean that topping up is all that is needed. The complete message matters.

AdBlue has its own tank and is used by the SCR system to reduce nitrogen oxide emissions. The fluid quantity, its delivery and dosing, and assessment of system efficiency are different parts of one system. An AdBlue message does not by itself identify a faulty part.

Why a warning may remain after topping up

If the problem was a low level, the vehicle must correctly recognise the top-up. The quantity required and the recognition conditions vary. Follow the instructions for your own vehicle; there is no single quantity or waiting time that applies across the whole Group.

With an SCR fault, however, a full tank does not prove that the system works correctly. Topping up will not fix, for example, a leaking line, a pumping fault or a sensing error. Do not repeatedly overfill the tank in an attempt to clear a fault message.

Why the extent of the repair varies

For the particular version, it is necessary to check which parts can be replaced separately according to the current parts catalogue and repair documentation. If a faulty item is supplied only as part of a larger assembly, that assembly may need replacing. This is not a rule for every tank or every AdBlue warning.

On some designs, the pump, sensing module or heater can also be replaced separately. Even the need to remove the tank to reach a component does not in itself mean that the entire tank must be replaced.

Clearing a message does not complete the repair

After the cause has been removed, the specified service operations and a functional check may follow, depending on the version. An update, a settings procedure and physical replacement of a control unit are different operations. Their necessity is not determined solely by the countdown on the instrument panel.

What to tell the workshop

For a quicker discussion, have the VIN, current mileage and a brief description of the requested work or fault ready. We confirm the appointment and scope of work by agreement.

What to prepare for the workshop

Report the full message, the remaining countdown and details of the last top-up: when it was done, how much fluid was added and whether the behaviour changed. Mention previous repairs and any possible connection with cold weather.

How the cause is checked

The checks go beyond the tank

Depending on the problem found, checks cover fluid delivery, leak-tightness, dosing, heating, sensing and electrical connections. Low pressure alone does not yet prove a faulty pump. A leaking supply line may be responsible, for example. Its condition therefore needs checking before a pump replacement is decided on.

Exhaust gas data is also used to assess SCR operation. A fault in a NOx sensor, its power supply or communication can affect the system. Dried AdBlue residue or a stored fault code provides a basis for checks, not a ready-made list of parts to order.
