Adaptive cruise control or emergency braking unavailable

An unavailable assistance function can indicate temporarily restricted sensing or a technical fault. Function names and capabilities depend on equipment.

What to do now

Take full responsibility for speed, distance and braking, and follow the exact message. When safely parked, check external sensor surfaces for dirt as permitted by the manual. Do not adjust sensors or their brackets.

Information to have ready

VIN, the exact message or a photograph, circumstances, and details of impacts or previous windscreen, bumper or suspension repairs.

The actual equipment and vehicle version determine the inspection scope and procedure.

Symptoms and inspection in detail

Weather and restricted sensor visibility

Snow, ice, mud or dirt can restrict radar or camera operation. Confirm sensor locations and cleaning instructions for the specific vehicle. A clean exterior surface alone does not resolve a persistent or recurring warning.

Diagnosis and calibration serve different purposes

Diagnosis identifies why the function is unavailable; calibration establishes the required sensor geometry using the manufacturer’s procedure. Clearing a fault is not calibration, and calibration cannot repair a damaged bracket, wiring or an unsuitable part.

The system and repair determine whether calibration is needed, its conditions and any preceding alignment. A procedure from a similar model cannot simply be reused.

Distinguish the assistance functions

Distance control and collision warning or emergency braking are different functions, even where they share sensors. One warning therefore does not establish the extent of a fault. If the system intervenes unexpectedly more than once, do not rely on it or deliberately trigger another intervention. Follow the vehicle handbook and arrange a professional check.
