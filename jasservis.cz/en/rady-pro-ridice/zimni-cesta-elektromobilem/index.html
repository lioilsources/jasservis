A winter journey in an electric car

  • Home
  • Driver advice
  • A winter journey in an electric car

Back to driver advice ←

In freezing weather, an electric car may use more energy and a cold battery may charge more slowly at a rapid charger. Plan the first stop with charge to spare and choose a backup charging station in advance.

Before you set off

Use a range estimate that reflects your route and the conditions. Motorway speeds, headwinds, freezing temperatures and cabin heating all affect range. Once you are under way, keep an eye on the predicted charge at your destination or next charger. If it drops quickly, choose an earlier stop.

If your car supports it, warm the cabin before departure while the car is still plugged in. The electricity supply can then provide some of the energy for heating.

Charging on the way

For both your planned and backup stops, check the connector, whether the station is in service and how to pay. Choose a backup you can still reach with charge to spare if the first charger is occupied or out of service.

Allow extra time on a winter journey. The power quoted on the charger is its maximum. The actual charging rate also depends on the battery temperature, its state of charge and your car’s charging capabilities.

Preparing the battery for rapid charging

Cabin preheating and preparing the traction battery for rapid direct-current (DC) charging are separate functions. Depending on the model and software version, battery preconditioning starts manually or automatically. Automatic preconditioning may, for example, require an active route that includes a rapid charger in the car’s built-in route planner.

Before you travel, check your vehicle handbook to see whether your car supports the feature and how to switch it on. If it is unavailable, allow a longer charging stop.
