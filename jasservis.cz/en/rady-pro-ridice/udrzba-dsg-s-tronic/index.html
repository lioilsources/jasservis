DSG and S tronic servicing – what does my gearbox need?

  • Home
  • Driver advice
  • DSG and S tronic servicing – what does my gearbox need?

Back to driver advice ←

Applicability depends on the engine, equipment and vehicle specification. Selecting a brand alone does not identify a particular vehicle.

Transmission oil servicing need not coincide with the engine oil service. Correct booking starts with identifying the actual gearbox and its maintenance history.

Which vehicles this advice covers

Volkswagen Group vehicles with a DSG or S tronic dual-clutch transmission, including applicable hybrids. This guide does not specify maintenance for tiptronic, multitronic or pure electric drive reduction gears.

The DSG name or number of gears is not enough

The same trade name covers different designs. Both dry-clutch and wet-clutch transmissions exist; six or seven speeds alone do not reliably identify the type. Dry clutches do not mean that the entire transmission operates without oil.

Identification requires the VIN, production data and the gearbox actually fitted. Tell the workshop if it has been replaced. Instructions for a similar model may not apply to your car.

When to change oil and filters

The maintenance schedule determines whether and when oil in a particular circuit needs changing. Filter replacement also depends on the design; it cannot be prescribed identically for every DSG. A single interval for all DSG and S tronic transmissions would be misleading.

Find the last service invoice showing the date, mileage and work performed. Without records, previous replacements cannot be confirmed. A routine inspection stamp alone may not document gearbox servicing.

What the service quotation should explain

The quotation should identify the gearbox, the oil circuit being serviced, the correct fluid and any required filter. Filling and level checks must follow the procedure and measurement conditions for that version.

Flushing, basic settings and adaptations are separate operations. Their need depends on the transmission, the work performed and the findings; they are not automatically mandatory parts of every oil change.

If the transmission jerks or displays a fault

Request fault assessment as well as maintenance when there is jerking, slipping or a warning. An oil change is not a confirmed repair for clutches, mechatronics or another cause. Record whether it happens cold or hot, on moving off, reversing or during a particular shift.

Photograph the complete message and retain diagnostic results before clearing them. Follow the exact message and vehicle handbook if drive is restricted; safe continuation cannot be decided from the transmission name.

What to prepare for the workshop

Provide the VIN, current mileage, previous maintenance records and a short description of the symptoms. Mention any replacement gearbox or software modification. These details help agree the appropriate maintenance or diagnostic work.
