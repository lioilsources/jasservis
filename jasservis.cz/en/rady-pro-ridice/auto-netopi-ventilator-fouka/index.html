The cabin heater blows cold air, but the fan works

  • Home
  • Driver advice
  • The cabin heater blows cold air, but the fan works

Back to driver advice ←

What to do now

In a safe place, check the temperature setting as described in the vehicle handbook. Note whether the air stays cold everywhere or only on one side and whether the engine warms up as usual. If the windows will not clear and visibility is unsafe, do not set off or pull over safely.

If a red cooling-system warning or STOP message appears, stop safely and do not continue driving. Follow the exact message and the owner's manual to decide whether to switch the engine off. Do not open a hot or pressurised expansion tank. If steam or hot coolant is escaping, do not open the bonnet either.

The fan runs, but the air from the vents stays cold even after the engine would normally have warmed up. This guide covers vehicles with a combustion engine that use engine coolant for cabin heating. Electric vehicles and plug-in hybrids may use different systems and must be assessed for the specific vehicle.

How we check the cause in the workshop

The first step is to distinguish slow engine warm-up, insufficient heat reaching the heater core and a problem directing heated air into the cabin. Depending on the symptom and vehicle design, the workshop can compare engine temperature data and temperatures at the inlet and outlet of the heater core, and check how the air-mixing flaps operate. Cold air from the vents alone does not establish a fault in the thermostat or the heater core.
