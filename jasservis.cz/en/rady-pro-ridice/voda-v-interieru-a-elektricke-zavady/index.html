Water inside the car and electrical faults

  • Home
  • Driver advice
  • Water inside the car and electrical faults

←Back to driver advice

A wet carpet, moisture in the luggage compartment and a new electrical problem can share a cause. Water does not have to appear beside the component that subsequently stops working.

Which vehicles this advice applies to

Volkswagen Group vehicles. Possible leak paths and electrical connection locations depend on body style, roof equipment and the vehicle's other features.

When you find water

Record where the water appeared and what preceded it. Rain, washing or use of the rear screen washer can provide useful clues. Photograph the area before drying it and record accompanying messages.

Do not continue driving if functions needed for safe vehicle control fail or the vehicle tells you to stop. Do not try to reproduce the fault by pouring water over the car. Agree the required checks with the workshop.

If the vehicle has been flooded, do not attempt to start it or activate the drive system. Arrange a professional inspection and recovery.

How water can reach wiring

On a vehicle with a sunroof, impaired drainage is one possibility. Blocked drains can allow water from the roof cassette into the cabin and electrical connections. A wet carpet can have other causes too; the entry point and actual water path matter.

A leaking washer hose is another possibility. Depending on body style, escaping fluid can reach connectors in the luggage compartment. Incorrect tailgate-closed information may then appear even though the original problem is not the lock itself.

Why drying may not be enough

Moisture can damage connector contacts and cause interrupted or unintended circuit connections. The fault may therefore appear away from the first wet area. Repair starts by identifying the water source and checking the affected route.

After fixing the leak, assess contacts and wiring. Repair scope depends on actual damage; connecting a new electrical component to damaged connections may leave the problem unresolved.

What to prepare for the workshop

Provide the VIN, photographs and exact display messages. Mention previous roof, glass, body or washer repairs and their timing relative to the first symptoms.

Following repair, check sealing and drainage as well as the affected circuits, according to the findings. The aim is to confirm that both the water entry path and the related fault have been resolved.
