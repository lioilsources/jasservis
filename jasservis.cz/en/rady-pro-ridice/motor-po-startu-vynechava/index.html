The engine misfires and shakes after starting

  • The engine misfires and shakes after starting

←Back to driver advice

Why the cause may remain after the spark plugs and ignition coils have been replaced

Which vehicles this advice applies to

Volkswagen Group petrol engines. The risk of deposits and the appropriate inspection method depend on the specific engine and injection system.

What to do now

When not to delay an engine inspection

If the engine warning light is flashing and the car jerks badly or loses power, stop safely, do not continue driving and contact roadside assistance. Misfires can damage the catalytic converter. Do not try to clear the fault by driving at high engine speeds. Engine warning light on or flashing. What now?

What the symptom may mean

After a cold start, the engine runs unevenly, the vehicle shakes and the engine warning light may come on. Diagnostics may record combustion misfires. That record does not, however, establish that an ignition coil or spark plug is faulty. The cause needs to be identified first.

Carbon deposits on the intake valves and in the intake ports are one possible cause. They may be associated with uneven idling after a cold start and intermittent misfires. If the problem persists after ignition components have been replaced, investigation of the cause needs to continue.

A misfire describes a combustion problem

The term 'ignition misfire' can lead to a search confined to the ignition system. However, the cause of irregular combustion may also lie in injection, electrical connections or the mechanical condition of the engine. A misfire report alone is therefore not a reason to replace coils, spark plugs or injectors across the board.

Why the injection system matters

In an engine with direct injection only, petrol is injected straight into the combustion chamber. The intake valves are therefore not washed by fuel as they are with port injection. Some engines combine both systems; port injection then helps clean the intake ports and valves. What matters is the actual engine design, not simply the TSI or TFSI badge. Even combined injection does not guarantee that deposits will never form.

Deposits on intake valves can restrict airflow and interfere with valve operation. This is not the same as deposits on an injector tip or a fouled spark plug. The different locations and their effects need to be distinguished during the inspection.

Cold starts and short journeys are important circumstances

Tell the workshop whether it happens after the car has been parked for a while, after short trips or when the engine has warmed up. Short journeys or smoother running once warm are insufficient to establish that the intake valves have deposits. In its bulletin for TFSI engines, Audi reports rough running after a cold start more often on cars mainly used for short trips.

A tank additive is not the same as cleaning the intake valves

A cleaner's effect on injectors or the combustion chamber cannot automatically be taken to mean that it removes deposits from the intake side of the valves. In an engine with direct injection only, fuel does not reach the intake valves by this route. A tank additive is therefore not a substitute for targeted treatment of confirmed valve deposits. The actual type of injection must be considered when assessing a product's effect.

Once the cause has been confirmed, we choose the intervention according to the specific engine and its current repair documentation. The aim is to remove the identified fault and then verify engine operation, rather than book cleaning solely because another vehicle had a similar story.

What to tell the workshop

What helps when investigating the cause

Circumstance Useful information for the workshop
Cold or warm engine Whether the problem occurs after the vehicle has been standing for a longer period, with a warm engine, or in both cases.
Duration and recurrence How long the uneven running lasts and whether it occurs after every start.
Driving pattern Mainly short journeys, longer trips and any changes in how the vehicle is used.
Previous repairs What has actually been replaced or checked, and whether this changed the symptom.
Changes before the problem began Any connection with refuelling, a repair or another change. This is a lead for investigation, not a confirmed diagnosis.
Engine warning light Whether it is steadily lit or flashing. Record the message or take a photograph only after stopping safely.

How the cause is checked

What diagnostics should establish

The inspection should take account of the conditions in which the engine misfires and the repairs already carried out. Depending on the findings, checks cover ignition, fuel supply and injection, electrical connections and the engine's mechanical condition. It is also important to verify the repair under conditions corresponding to the original symptom.

If carbon deposits are suspected, we need to establish both their presence and their connection with the uneven running. A photograph or borescope image of deposits alone does not prove that they are causing the particular misfire. A finding in the combustion chamber is also not the same as the condition of the intake side of the valves.

New spark plugs and ignition coils do not, on their own, confirm carbon build-up either. Depending on the results, further functional or mechanical checks may be needed. More than one fault cannot be ruled out without investigation.
