Humming from a wheel while driving

  • Home

  • Humming from a wheel while driving

Back to driver advice ←

You may hear the hum at a steady speed without braking. If the sound is new or getting louder, have the car checked. Tell the workshop when you first noticed it.

What to do if you hear a hum from a wheel

If you notice sudden strong vibrations, a change in steering, reduced braking performance, smoke or a burning smell, pull over safely and do not continue driving. Call roadside assistance as appropriate. If smoke persists, or if anything is smouldering or on fire, move away from the vehicle and traffic and call 150 or 112 in Czechia. Avoid touching a hot wheel or brake.

For more guidance, see One wheel is unusually hot or smells of burning.

Tell the workshop whether the hum changed with speed, road surface or when going round bends in normal driving, and whether it was audible without braking. Mention any recent tyre change or impact with a pothole. Avoid deliberately changing speed or direction to reproduce it. The side the sound seems to come from inside the cabin may differ from the side where the problem is.

How a workshop checks for the cause

Depending on the symptoms, the workshop checks for uneven tyre wear, such as a step between tread blocks. Depending on the design, the wheel bearing can be checked for noise when turning and for play. If brake drag is suspected, the workshop checks whether the wheel turns freely once the brake is released. Changes with speed or in bends help guide the inspection. The cause is established from the findings on the vehicle.
