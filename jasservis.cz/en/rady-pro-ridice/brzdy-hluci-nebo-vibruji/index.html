Brake noise or vibration

Squealing, scraping and vibration under braking have different causes. Sound alone cannot identify a faulty part or establish whether it is safe to continue driving.

What to do now

If braking performance deteriorates, the pedal sinks unusually, the vehicle pulls strongly under braking or a STOP instruction appears, stop safely and arrange professional assistance. Do not test the brakes by repeatedly braking hard in traffic.

For the right action when the pedal feels different or one wheel is overheating, see The brake pedal suddenly feels hard or sinks and One wheel is unusually hot or smells of burning.

Information to have ready

VIN, when and at what speed the symptom occurs, whether the brakes are cold or warm, and details of recent brake or wheel work.

Arrange a vehicle check

The actual equipment and vehicle version determine the inspection scope and procedure.

Symptoms and inspection in detail

Noise after parking or a persistent problem?

Moisture and surface corrosion can change brake noise after parking. Persistent scraping, recurring squealing or a change in braking performance require inspection. Adequate thickness of the outer pad does not establish the condition of the whole brake.

What does vibration under braking mean?

The inspection distinguishes vibration in the pedal, steering wheel and body, and its relationship to speed and temperature. It may include disc condition and thickness, lateral runout, hub mounting surfaces, pads and caliper operation. Measurement methods and limits depend on the specific brake system.

Vibration alone does not confirm “warped discs”. Wheels and suspension must also be considered; replacing discs alone may not remove the cause.

Electric and hybrid vehicles

Regenerative braking can reduce use of the friction brakes. Less wear does not automatically mean good condition: corrosion and free movement also need checking. Maintenance depends on the actual disc or drum brake design and manufacturer instructions.
