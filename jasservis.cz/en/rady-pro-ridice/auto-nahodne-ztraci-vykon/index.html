The car intermittently loses power

  • The car intermittently loses power

←Back to driver advice

Applicability depends on the engine, equipment and vehicle version. Selecting a brand alone does not identify the exact vehicle.

When we look for damaged vacuum hoses and wiring

Which vehicles this advice applies to

Volkswagen Group vehicles, with a focus on 2.0 TDI engines. The control design, routing and diagnostic conditions depend on the particular engine and vehicle generation.

What to do now

What to do if power suddenly drops

Do not put unnecessary load on the vehicle, and stop safely as traffic conditions allow. Do not repeatedly try to reproduce the loss of power at full throttle, and do not switch off the engine while driving. Discuss the next steps with the workshop according to the symptoms and vehicle warnings. If its condition does not allow safe driving, arrange roadside assistance.

What the symptom may mean

On one journey the vehicle pulls normally, while on another it becomes noticeably less powerful or responds to the accelerator differently from usual. This symptom alone does not identify a faulty part. On some engines, a leaking vacuum hose can affect performance; in other cases, damaged wiring or a faulty contact may be involved.

In our work at J&S SERVIS, we also check for chewed hoses and damaged wiring when investigating similar problems. We encounter these findings particularly on customers' 2.0 TDI vehicles. This is the workshop's experience, not a rule that a marten causes every intermittent loss of power on this engine.

How a small hose can affect engine performance

On versions with vacuum actuation, vacuum helps move the relevant mechanism. If the control circuit leaks, regulation may not work correctly. Damaged hoses, including bite damage, or leaking connections may be responsible; the result may be insufficient power or limp operation.

During checks, we distinguish the vacuum lines used for control from the charge-air pipework carrying air into the engine. These are separate circuits. Depending on the version, a leak in either can produce a similar reduction in power.

The same engine designation does not mean the same control system

Depending on the design, turbocharger actuation may be mechanical-pneumatic or electromechanical. The actual engine version must therefore be identified first. An electrically controlled valve in a vacuum system is not the same as an electric actuator that directly moves the turbocharger mechanism.

Damaged wiring can resemble another fault

Electrical signals and the power supply also matter for control. A damaged wire or contact at the boost pressure sensor can cause low power or juddering. Replacing the turbocharger will not repair a wiring fault.

An electrical fault may be intermittent. On a particular Audi version, vibration can damage wires and cause intermittent or permanent loss of communication with a sensor. This does not imply the same fault on every 2.0 TDI engine or damage by an animal. The cause must be verified on the particular vehicle.An electrical fault may occur intermittently. Its cause needs to be verified on the specific vehicle. An occasional loss of power alone does not mean that all 2.0 TDI engines have the same fault, nor does it prove animal damage.

An intermittent symptom does not mean an arbitrary cause

The fault needs to be assessed under the operating conditions in which it occurs. Vacuum problems can affect performance at different loads. This does not mean that a damaged hose leaks only during acceleration: the leak and the point when the driver feels its effect are two different things.

If normal power returns after a later engine start, tell the workshop. The return of power does not confirm that the cause has been removed and is not a reason to stop investigating the fault.

What to tell the workshop

For a quicker discussion, have the VIN, current mileage and a brief description of the requested work or fault ready. We confirm the appointment and scope of work by agreement.

What to record when power is lost

Circumstance Useful information for the workshop
Driving conditions Whether power was lacking during acceleration, steady driving, uphill or after warming up.
Engine behaviour Whether the vehicle simply lost power, juddered, sounded unusual or showed other accompanying symptoms.
Instrument panel warnings The complete message and warning lights; take a photograph only after stopping safely.
How the fault develops How often it returns and whether it followed a long period of standing, a repair or other changes.
A visible finding Signs of animals, a damaged hose or wire. The finding helps the inspection but does not by itself establish the cause of the power loss.

How the cause is checked

What the checks must establish

Depending on the version, we check hoses, connections, actuation and the related wiring. For wiring, we distinguish damaged insulation, a broken conductor and a faulty contact. We establish whether the damage actually causes the reported problems through inspection, diagnostic data and appropriate measurements, rather than the appearance of one component alone.

A fault code relating to boost pressure control is not in itself an instruction to replace the turbocharger. Conversely, the absence of a relevant code does not by itself rule out an electrical fault. After repair, the affected circuit must be checked for correct operation and, where possible, the vehicle's behaviour checked under the conditions of the original power loss.
