What to do now

If misted windows prevent a clear view, stop safely. Follow your vehicle’s handbook to restore visibility; do not repeatedly try to reproduce the fault while driving.

When an icing evaporator may be the cause

Which vehicles this advice applies to

Volkswagen Group vehicles with air conditioning. The design, control system and repair procedure depend on the model and equipment.

What the symptom may mean

The air conditioning works at first, then its performance weakens and returns after a break. With this behaviour, it is important to distinguish between two things: whether less air is coming from the vents, or whether the air simply stops being cold.

On some versions, an intermittent reduction in airflow may be related to evaporator icing. However, alternating periods of normal operation and loss of function do not, on their own, identify the cause or the part that needs replacing.

How ice can form

The evaporator cools the air for the cabin, and moisture in the air condenses on its surface. If this condensation freezes, ice can restrict airflow through the evaporator. The fan may still be audible while less air comes from the vents. Airflow may return once the ice has melted; the return to normal operation alone does not, however, confirm icing.

Depending on the design, a fault in refrigerant circuit control, an incorrectly positioned temperature sensor or restricted airflow through a clogged cabin filter may contribute to icing. A similar symptom therefore does not have one common cause across all vehicles.

Weak airflow and warm air are different symptoms

An audible fan with little air coming from the vents does not, on its own, prove icing. The fan and correct air distribution by the flaps also need to be checked. The actual symptom determines which part should be investigated.

If the air volume remains similar and it is mainly the temperature that changes, we also investigate the refrigerant circuit and the mixing of warm and cold air. This is a different starting point from a gradual reduction in airflow.

The sensor is one possibility, not an automatic conclusion

Depending on the design, the evaporator temperature sensor may affect evaporator control. If it is suspected, the plausibility of the measured temperature, the sensor's position and its electrical connection are checked. A faulty sensor is one possible cause, not a reason to replace it automatically whenever the air conditioning stops working.

Icing may also be related to compressor control or regulation. The same outward symptom may therefore require a different repair. The identified fault and the documentation for the specific vehicle determine the next steps.

What to tell the workshop

Provide the VIN and current mileage, together with the following details:

What to notice when the problem occurs

Observation What to tell the workshop
Air volume Whether airflow gradually weakens or remains similar to the level before the problem occurred.
Fan noise Whether you can still hear the fan and how it responds to changes in the settings.
Timing and conditions How long you had been driving when the problem began; the weather and the temperature, fan and recirculation settings.
Return to normal operation Whether cooling returned on its own or after a break, and whether the problem recurs.

Why a description of the conditions helps

The air conditioning may be working correctly when the vehicle reaches the workshop. The circumstances listed in the table are therefore useful when arranging an inspection, particularly the distinction between air volume and air temperature. Make notes or photograph the settings only after stopping safely.

A temporary return to normal operation does not confirm a repair. The aim is to establish what changes when the problem occurs, then check the relevant part of the system. Where possible, an intermittent fault is therefore assessed while it is actually present.

How the cause is checked

What a professional inspection should establish

We first check the actual symptom and the associated data, then the airflow and, depending on the findings, the refrigerant circuit. The inspection should consider how the entire system works. Simply adding refrigerant does not establish the cause of a recurring problem; depending on the findings, the correct charge and system tightness also need to be checked.

The outcome should be an explanation of the specific cause and verification that the system works after the repair. The fact that the air conditioning happens to be cooling in the workshop is not enough to close a recurring fault.
