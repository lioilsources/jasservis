The headlamp fogs up inside or contains water

  • Home

  • The headlamp fogs up inside or contains water

Back to driver advice ←

A light mist can form inside a headlamp after rain, washing or a change in temperature. If moisture affects the lamp and you lack the lighting needed for safe driving, do not set off. If the fault develops while driving, stop safely and arrange help.

What to do if a headlamp mists up or contains water

A thin mist that clears by itself as conditions change can be normal condensation. Visible droplets, pooled water or mist that does not clear should be checked.

With the vehicle safely parked, photograph the affected area and note whether it appeared after washing, rain or with no obvious trigger. Record which lamp is affected and whether it shows a fault or works differently. Do not drill into or take apart the headlamp to dry it.

How the workshop checks the cause

Checks depend on the design and cover the vents, rear cover, seals, and damage to the housing or lens. On lamps with controlled airflow, its operation can also be checked. If there is a fault message or the light does not work correctly, the findings may also call for checks of electrical connections. Mist on the lens alone does not show where water entered.
