When to change the engine oil

  • When to change the engine oil

←Back to driver advice

Applicability depends on the engine, equipment and vehicle version. Selecting a brand alone does not identify the exact vehicle.

How the way you use your vehicle affects its service plan

Which vehicles this advice applies to

Combustion engines, including the combustion engine in a hybrid drivetrain. A fully electric vehicle does not require a combustion-engine oil service.

What to do now

Oil level, oil pressure and the change date are different things

Topping up a low oil level does not replace a scheduled oil change. Check the level as specified for the vehicle and do not overfill it. A correct level alone does not confirm adequate lubrication pressure.

A red oil pressure warning is not a service reminder. Stop safely and switch off the engine. Follow the exact message and the handbook when deciding on the next steps; if uncertain, arrange professional assistance. The distance remaining until the oil change does not determine the cause of the warning.

What the symptom may mean

The timing of an engine oil change depends on more than distance alone. The starting point is the service requirements for the particular engine, the selected service regime and how the vehicle is used.

Two cars covering the same annual distance may be used very differently: one regularly makes long journeys, while the other mainly takes short trips around town. When planning maintenance, describe everyday use rather than just the total distance.

Fixed and flexible schedules have their own conditions

Frequent short journeys, urban use or certain types of higher loading may call for a different regime from long, steady journeys with a lighter load. Whether there is a choice must be checked for that vehicle.

The word ‘motorway’ alone does not describe driving conditions. A lightly loaded vehicle on a steady journey operates differently from a vehicle towing a trailer and repeatedly working under a high load. One longer holiday trip does not replace an assessment of everyday use.

Viscosity and the LongLife label are not enough on their own

The oil must meet the manufacturer's required specification and approval. When choosing it, compare the oil's labelling with the requirement for the engine stated in the handbook or the appropriate vehicle information. The same viscosity, a familiar brand or a higher price do not by themselves confirm suitability.

LongLife on an oil container does not determine the next change date or change the service regime by itself. The timing follows the requirements and selected schedule for the particular vehicle. It is not generally true that a longer approved interval always damages the engine, nor that it can be used on every car.

What to tell the workshop

For a quicker discussion, have the VIN, current mileage and a brief description of the requested work or fault ready. We confirm the appointment and scope of work by agreement.

What to check before an oil service

What you have noticed What to report or distinguish
The last oil change The date and odometer reading; tell the workshop if a used vehicle's history is unverified.
Service regime A fixed or flexible schedule and the requirements of the particular vehicle.
Oil used The required approval and suitability for that engine, not just viscosity.
Normal use Journey lengths, city driving, towing and repeated high loads.

How the cause is checked

Distinguish service requirements from preventive recommendations

After assessing how the vehicle is used, the workshop may recommend an earlier preventive oil change. The reason should be explained, and the recommendation should be clearly distinguished from a mandatory manufacturer service requirement.

An earlier change does not replace other required maintenance. An oil service and an inspection may have different due dates. The oil filter is also dealt with according to the applicable requirements. Keep a record of the date, odometer reading and oil used.
