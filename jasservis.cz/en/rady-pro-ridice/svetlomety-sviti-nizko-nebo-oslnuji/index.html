How headlamp levelling relates to a ride-height sensor and the suspension

Which vehicles this advice applies to

Vehicles with automatic headlamp levelling. Any connection with electronically controlled damping or air suspension depends on the model and equipment.Vehicles with automatic headlight range control. Integration with electronically controlled damping depends on the model and equipment.

What to do now

If the headlights do not allow you to see the road safely in the conditions, or significant dazzling cannot be prevented, stop safely and discuss the next steps. Do not try to compensate for the fault by driving with main beam permanently on.

What the symptom may mean

Dipped headlights may aim noticeably too high and dazzle other drivers, or illuminate only a short stretch ahead. If automatic headlamp levelling has stopped working correctly, the headlamp itself may not be the cause. The ride-height sensor, its mechanical linkage and the electrical part of the system are also checked.

A change in the aim of the dipped headlights

Fallback behaviour is not the same on every vehicle. On some, the headlights remain in their previous position when a fault occurs; on others, they move to a default position. It is therefore not generally true that every fault always lowers the lights fully.

What the ride-height sensor measures

The sensor monitors the position of the body relative to the axle or a related suspension component. On a lever-operated version, movement is converted through a lever and linkage into an electrical signal for the control system. Depending on the design, that information is used for headlamp levelling or other suspension functions.

A damaged or disconnected linkage, an incorrectly positioned connection, a faulty sensor or its wiring can cause problems. Visible linkage damage does not by itself confirm an electronic fault inside the sensor.

The connection with a suspension fault

On some designs, the headlights and electronically controlled suspension share information about the body's position. A fault affecting that shared input may result in both inoperative headlamp levelling and restricted damping or air suspension control. The effect depends on the wiring and equipment of the particular vehicle.On some designs, the headlights and electronically controlled suspension share body position data. A fault in a shared input of this kind may result in both inoperative headlight range control and restricted damping control. The extent of the effect depends on the system layout and equipment of the specific vehicle.

Electronic damper control adjusts the damping characteristic. DCC, for example, controls the damper valves using sensor data. What to tell the workshop

For a quicker discussion, have the VIN, current mileage and a brief description of the requested work or fault ready. We confirm the appointment and scope of work by agreement.

What exactly to describe when contacting the workshop

Distinguish a problem with dipped headlights from one occurring during automatic use of main beam. Headlamp levelling and main-beam or adaptive assistance functions require different assessment. Also report whether the vehicle's height or suspension behaviour has changed or the problem appeared after work on the suspension or a headlamp.

How the cause is checked

What needs checking

First, the system fitted to the vehicle must be identified and the exact message recorded. The sensor's mechanical linkage, installation, wiring and diagnostic data are then checked. Depending on the results, the headlamp actuators and settings are also examined. The extent of the checks depends on the particular vehicle.

It is important to compare the measured or displayed position with the actual suspension condition. A sensor name appearing in a fault report does not in itself determine whether its body needs replacing, the linkage needs repairing or another cause needs addressing.

Why simply raising the lights manually is not enough

Mechanically readjusting a headlamp does not verify the operation of automatic levelling. After the cause is removed, the necessary basic settings, adjustment and functional check are carried out as specified for the particular system.

Dazzling can also result from an incorrect previous adjustment procedure. It is therefore necessary to check not just the sensor, but also the initial state of the levelling system and the correct headlamp setting.
