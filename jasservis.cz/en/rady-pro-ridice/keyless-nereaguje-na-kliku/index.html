Keyless access does not respond to touching the handle

  • Home

  • Keyless access does not respond to touching the handle

←Back to driver advice

Applicability depends on the engine, equipment and vehicle version. Selecting a brand alone does not identify the exact vehicle.

How to distinguish the operating method, a temporary restriction and a fault

Which vehicles this advice applies to

Volkswagen Group vehicles equipped with keyless access. The handles equipped for keyless access, sensing surfaces and functional restrictions depend on the particular version.

What to do now

Dirt, snow and salt have a documented effect

The manufacturer warns that dirt, snow and salt can affect the electrical properties around the handle and correct sensor operation. If the surface is dirty, gentle cleaning in accordance with the care instructions for the particular vehicle is therefore worthwhile.

If operation returns, record that as well. If the problem repeats, cleaning alone may not explain the cause. Do not interfere with damaged or internal parts of the handle yourself.

What the symptom may mean

You grip the handle and the door remains locked, although the key button still unlocks it. This symptom must be distinguished from complete central locking failure. Working remote-control buttons do not in themselves confirm correct keyless access operation.

Assessment depends on the condition of the sensing surfaces, how they are touched and recognition of an authorised key. Cleaning may help if dirt is present, but repeated problems cannot be dismissed as a normal characteristic without checking.

Touch detection and key recognition are two separate conditions

In the relevant systems, the handle sensors work on a capacitive principle: they detect a change as a hand approaches. At the same time, the vehicle must recognise an authorised key. A failure therefore need not originate solely in the handle itself.

A low key or vehicle battery, interference or some settings can affect operation. Trying a second key helps investigate the cause, but does not by itself determine what needs replacing.

Moisture and cold are not a diagnosis

Water on the surface is different from water entering the sensor. In some older systems, such ingress can cause intermittent failures. On other versions, cold-weather problems may relate to the handle and its surface finish.

Ordinary rain cannot therefore automatically be treated as the cause of a failure in an otherwise sound system. Restored function after drying does not confirm that the fault has been removed either. A connection with moisture or cold helps guide the checks, but does not identify a part to replace. During washing, a water jet may also activate the sensing surface if an authorised key is nearby.

Unlocking and locking may use different surfaces

On some versions, touching the inside of the handle unlocks the door, while touching a designated outer surface locks it. Gripping the handle and touching both surfaces at once can prevent locking. Pulling too quickly may also give the system insufficient time to recognise the key. Check the precise operating method in your vehicle's handbook.

A brief block on unlocking again after locking may be intentional, allowing you to check that the vehicle is locked. Some generations limit sensing after a longer period of standing, while others do not behave this way. It is therefore not possible to prescribe one waiting time or the same restoration procedure for every car.

What to tell the workshop

For a quicker discussion, have the VIN, current mileage and a brief description of the requested work or fault ready. We confirm the appointment and scope of work by agreement.

What is useful to distinguish

Observed symptom What to tell the workshop
The buttons work but the handle does not respond Whether unlocking, locking or both are affected.
One or more handles do not respond Which doors are affected. Compare only handles actually equipped for keyless operation.
Behaviour varies with the key or location Whether the same symptom occurs with the second key and whether it depends on where the vehicle is parked.
A connection with weather or washing The surface condition, rain, snow, cold and any change after normal cleaning or a change in weather.
A fault message or a problem after standing for a longer period The exact message and how long the vehicle stood. These circumstances do not by themselves identify a faulty component.

How the cause is checked

When a professional check is needed

If the problem returns on a clean surface, depends strongly on the weather or the vehicle reports a fault, the system should be checked according to its version. Checks may include sensors, wiring, key recognition and related diagnostic data.

The number of unresponsive handles alone does not identify the cause either. On one older version, a single faulty sensor can restrict access through other doors as well. Similarly, an empty fault memory does not rule out every real problem. Describe the particular action and circumstances to the workshop rather than just naming a part selected in advance.
