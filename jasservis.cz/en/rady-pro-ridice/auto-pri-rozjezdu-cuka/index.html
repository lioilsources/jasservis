The car judders when pulling away

  • Home
  • Driver advice
  • The car judders when pulling away

Back to driver advice

Applicability depends on the engine, equipment and vehicle specification. Selecting a brand alone does not identify a particular vehicle.

What to observe and why an accurate description matters

Vehicles with a combustion engine and a manual or automatic gearbox, including DSG and S tronic. The checks required depend on the design of the drivetrain.

The car shudders when pulling away, jerks several times or takes up drive differently from before. The symptom alone does not identify a faulty component. The problem may arise as the clutch engages, from an uneven-running engine or from related parts of the drivetrain.

For diagnosis, it is important to distinguish a single jolt from repeated juddering or a change during a gearshift. An accurate description helps determine which parts need checking.

Which circumstances help the workshop

What you have noticed What to report or distinguish
Cold or warm vehicle Whether the juddering occurs only after starting, once warm or in both situations.
Pulling away forwards or reversing Whether the symptom is the same in both directions.
Manual gearbox Whether the juddering occurs as you release the clutch pedal or continues after the pedal is fully released.
Automatic gearbox Whether there is a jolt when selecting a driving mode, when pulling away or only during a gearshift.
Other symptoms Uneven engine running while stationary, noise and the exact accompanying message.

When to stop trying to pull away

If the vehicle pulls away unpredictably, no longer transmits drive reliably or cannot be controlled safely, do not repeatedly try to reproduce the fault. Stop safely and discuss the next steps with the workshop or roadside assistance. Follow any explicit instruction from the vehicle to stop.

Milder juddering without a warning light does not in itself confirm that it is safe to continue driving. Seek advice about any new or worsening symptom. A description based on normal use is enough; do not carry out load tests to confirm the fault.

Why replacing a part is not the starting point

If juddering occurs as the clutch engages, the clutch, engine and gearbox mounts and other related parts are checked as indicated by the findings. Noise from the gearbox area does not by itself confirm damage to the dual-mass flywheel either. The possible source of the symptom must first be distinguished.

On some versions, the control software may also play a part. We therefore also check whether a suitable software update is available for the particular vehicle. A similar symptom does not mean the same repair is needed on every car.

How the cause is established

The workshop starts with the vehicle's exact specification, the description of the problem and its maintenance and repair history. As needed, diagnostic records and operating values are assessed alongside a mechanical inspection. A road test is appropriate only if the vehicle's condition allows it.

Oil changes have their own service requirements. Juddering alone is not evidence that changing the fluid will cure the problem. Any basic settings procedure or software update must also suit the identified cause and the procedure for that gearbox.

What to prepare before contacting the workshop

Note the approximate mileage, when the problem began and any connection with recent servicing. A useful description might be: ‘After a morning start, the car jerks twice when first pulling away, but moves off smoothly once warm.’ Note the exact message or report it to the workshop after stopping.
